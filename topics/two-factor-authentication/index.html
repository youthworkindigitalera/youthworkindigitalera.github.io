01 · What is 2FA?

A second lockon your account.

Two-factor authentication (2FA) is an extra layer of security used to ensure that people trying to gain access to an online account are who they say they are.

First, you enter your username and password. Then, instead of immediately gaining access, you must provide another piece of information (like a code sent to your phone or a fingerprint).

02 · Why it matters

Even if they stealyour password.

Two-factor authentication is vital because it adds a second layer of defense, making it nearly impossible for hackers to access your accounts with just a password.

Even if hackers steal your password, they cannot access your account without your physical device. This simple setup keeps your identity, money, and personal data completely safe.

  1. 1Password

    You enter your username and password.

  2. 2Second factor

    A code is sent to your phone, or you use your fingerprint.

  3. 3Access

    Only you, with your device, can get in.

03 · Story

The codeshe didn’t ask for.

A fictional story based on a common real-world situation.

Mia, 17, used the same password for her email and an old gaming account. One night, her phone buzzed: “Your login code is 615 204.” But Mia wasn’t trying to log in.

A few minutes later, a message arrived from “Support”: “We sent you a code by mistake. Please send it back to us so we can cancel the request.”

Mia remembered the workshop: nobody real ever asks for your code. She didn’t reply. Instead she opened her email app herself, changed her password, and signed out of all other devices.

Someone really did have her old password. But because two-factor authentication was on, they never got in.

The lessonA code you didn’t ask for is an alarm bell: someone has your password. Don’t share the code. Change your password.

04 · Your options

Which secondlock should I use?

  • GoodSMS code

    A code is sent to your phone by text message. Much better than nothing, but phone numbers can be hijacked through “SIM swapping”.

  • BetterAuthenticator app

    An app such as Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator creates a new code every 30 seconds, right on your phone.

  • EasyFingerprint or face

    Your phone confirms it’s really you. Many apps and passkeys use this as the second step.

  • StrongestSecurity key

    A small USB or NFC key you plug in or tap. It can’t be tricked by fake websites.

05 · How to turn it on

Five minutesper account.

  1. Open the security settings

    In the app or website, look for Settings › Security (or “Password and security”).

  2. Find two-factor or two-step

    It may be called two-factor authentication, two-step verification or 2FA.

  3. Choose an authenticator app

    If you can, pick the app instead of SMS. Scan the QR code it shows you.

  4. Type in the code

    Enter the 6-digit code from the app to confirm that it works.

  5. Save your backup codes

    Write them down or keep them in your password manager. They help if you lose your phone.

  6. Repeat for every important account

    Start with your email, then banking, then social media and messaging apps.
