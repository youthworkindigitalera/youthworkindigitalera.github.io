01 · Introduction

You leave traceseverywhere.

Everything you post, like, share or search leaves a trace online. Anyone with patience and free tools can collect those traces and build a picture of your life. This page shows how that works and how to take back control.

02 · Story

How a stranger foundLaura in 20 minutes.

Laura is 18. Her Instagram is public, but she thinks she never shares anything private.

One evening she posts a selfie with her new puppy. In the background you can see her school logo on a hoodie. Her bio says “Riga | class of 2026 | 🎸”. Last week she posted a story from her favourite café, tagged with location, saying “same place every Friday ☕”.

A stranger looks through her profile. From the hoodie he learns her school. From the café tag he knows where she is every Friday. A photo from her birthday shows the house number behind her. Her old TikTok account, with the same username, shows her full name and her mother’s name in the comments. A quick search of that username finds a 3-year-old forum post with her phone number.

In 20 minutes, without hacking anything, the stranger knows her full name, school, address, phone number and weekly routine.

The pointNothing was stolen. Laura shared every piece herself, one post at a time. This is how OSINT works.

03 · Explanations

Footprintsand OSINT.

What is a digital footprint?

It is all the data you leave behind when you use the internet. It stays online for years, often even after you delete it, because others may have screenshots or copies.

What is OSINT?

OSINT means Open Source Intelligence: collecting information that is publicly available and putting the pieces together. Journalists and police use it to investigate. Scammers, stalkers and bullies use the same methods to target people.

TypeWhat it meansExamples
Active footprintThings you share on purposePosts, photos, comments, bio, reviews
Passive footprintThings collected about you without you noticingLocation data, cookies, app tracking, photos others tag you in

Why it matters

  • Scammers use your details to make fake messages look real (they know your school, pet’s name, friends).
  • Pet names, birthdays and hometowns are often answers to password security questions.
  • Employers and universities check candidates online before hiring or accepting them.
  • Your location and routine can put your physical safety at risk.

Common clues people miss

  • School or work logos
  • House numbers
  • Car plates
  • Views from windows
  • Location tags
  • The same username on every platform
  • Posting “I’m away on holiday” in real time
