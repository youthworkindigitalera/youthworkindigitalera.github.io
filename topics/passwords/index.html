01 · Story

One password,five doors.

Tom was 14 and had one password for everything: tom2011. His name and his birth year. Easy to remember. Same for his games, his email and his Instagram.

One Monday, a small gaming site he had forgotten about got hacked. The hackers didn’t care about the game. They took the list of emails and passwords and tried them everywhere else.

By Tuesday, someone else was logged in as Tom. His friends got messages from “him” asking for gift card codes. His email password was changed. He couldn’t get back in.

Tom’s mistake wasn’t one weak password. It was one key for five doors. When a thief copies that key, every door opens.

It took Tom a week, and a lot of help, to get his accounts back. Then he did what the rest of this page explains. Now one leak can only ever open one door.

02 · Frequently asked questions

Your questions,answered.

A Reddit user wrote: “How do I make a secure password? How long should it be? I’m changing all my passwords and putting them into LastPass.” Here are the answers.

How long should a password be?

At least 15 characters. That is the current advice from NIST, the US agency that writes security standards. Length is the single most important thing. Every extra character makes a password much harder to guess.

Do I need symbols and numbers?

They help, but length matters more. NIST no longer says passwords must contain special characters. A long password with only words beats a short one full of symbols. Tr0ub!e is short and weak. purple tractor eats seventeen clouds is long and strong.

What is a passphrase?

A passphrase is 4 or more random words joined together. It is long, hard to guess, and easy to picture in your head. Pick words that don’t belong together. Avoid song lyrics, famous quotes and anything about you.

What should I never use?

Your name, birthday, pet, school or favourite team. Simple patterns like 123456, qwerty or password. A password you already use somewhere else.

Why not use the same password everywhere?

Because of Tom’s story. When one site is hacked, criminals try the stolen email and password on many other sites. A different password for every account means one leak opens only one door.

Is a password manager like LastPass a good idea?

Yes. A password manager is an app that keeps all your passwords in a locked, encrypted vault. You remember only one master password. The app creates long random passwords for you and fills them in. It also won’t fill your login into a fake look-alike website.

Three things to know: Your master password is everything: make it your longest, strongest passphrase and use it nowhere else. It usually can’t be recovered: the company can’t see it either, so keep a backup somewhere safe at home. No service is perfect: LastPass itself had a serious data breach in 2022. A long master password and two-step verification are what keep your vault safe even then.

What else protects me?

Turn on two-step verification (2FA or MFA). After your password, you also confirm with your phone. NIST calls this the first thing you should do. Even if someone steals your password, they still can’t get in. An authenticator app is safer than SMS codes.

Some sites now offer passkeys. They replace the password with your phone’s fingerprint or face unlock. They can’t be stolen by fake websites. Use them when you see the option.

Where do I start when changing everything?

Start with your email. Whoever controls your email can reset all your other passwords. Then do banking and payment apps, then social media, then everything else.

Statement A: length matters much more than complexity; a longer, simple passphrase gives you much higher security than a short password filled with symbols.
