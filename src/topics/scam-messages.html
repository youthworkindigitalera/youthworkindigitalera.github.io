01 · Introduction

What arescam messages?

Scam messages are fake text messages designed to trick people into revealing personal information, giving away banking details, or sending money to criminals.

Two common examples are fake bank SMS messages and fake courier messages. Scammers pretend to represent trusted companies, banks, or delivery services to make their messages look genuine.

They often use fear, urgency, or tempting offers to make people act without thinking. A single careless click can lead to stolen information, financial loss, or unauthorized access to an account.

The good news is that recognizing warning signs and checking messages carefully can help us stay safe.

02 · Real-life story

Anna’s almostcostly mistake.

{{ICON:info}} This is a fictional story based on common real-world scam techniques.

Anna is a 19-year-old student who recently ordered a pair of shoes online. One afternoon, while studying at home, she receives a text message from an unknown number.

Scene 1

The fake courier message

Unknown number · now

Your parcel is waiting for delivery. An unpaid fee of €1.99 is required. Pay now using the link below to avoid returning your parcel.

Anna is expecting a package, so the message seems believable. She thinks that €1.99 is a small amount and considers paying it immediately.

Just before clicking the link, she notices that the message comes from an unfamiliar number and pressures her to act quickly.

Instead of clicking, Anna opens the courier’s official website herself and checks her tracking information. She discovers that her parcel is already on its way and that no additional payment is required.

She realizes that the text was probably a scam.

Scene 2

The fake bank message

Later that evening, Anna receives another message:

“Your Bank” · 21:47

URGENT! Your bank account will be suspended today. Verify your identity immediately using this link.

Anna becomes frightened because she has money in her bank account. The message uses her bank’s name and looks professional.

She remembers what happened earlier and decides not to click the link. Instead, she opens her bank’s official mobile application.

Her account appears normal, so she contacts the bank using the official phone number listed on its website. The bank confirms that the suspicious message did not come from them.

Anna reports the message and blocks the sender.

Scene 3

What Anna learned

Anna understands that scammers try to make people panic and act quickly. They use believable situations, familiar company names, and urgent warnings to gain trust.

By checking both messages through official channels, Anna avoids losing money or exposing her personal information.

The lessonNever let fear or urgency make decisions for you. Stop, check, and think before you click.

03 · Fake courier messages

“Your parcelis waiting…”

How does this scam work?

In a fake courier scam, criminals pretend to be delivery companies or postal services. They may claim that a parcel is delayed, an address is incorrect, or an extra fee must be paid.

Their goal may be to steal payment card details, personal information, or login credentials.

Example of a fake courier SMS

Unknown number · now

Your package could not be delivered. Pay a small redelivery fee through this link to receive your parcel.

{{ICON:flag}} Warning signs

  • You receive an unexpected payment request.
  • The sender uses an unfamiliar phone number or suspicious website address.
  • The message threatens to return or hold your parcel.
  • You are pressured to act immediately.
  • The link does not match the courier’s official website.

{{ICON:shield}} How to protect yourself

  1. Check your tracking information through the courier’s official website or app.
  2. Do not open unexpected payment links.
  3. Contact the courier through its verified contact details if you are unsure.
  4. Never enter card details on a website reached through a suspicious message.
  5. Report the message to the relevant delivery company or mobile provider.

04 · Fake bank SMS messages

“Your accountwill be blocked…”

How does this scam work?

In a fake bank SMS scam, criminals pretend to represent a bank. They may claim that an account has been blocked, an unusual transaction has occurred, or personal details need to be verified.

The message often contains a link to a fake website that imitates a real bank’s login page.

If someone enters their credentials or verification codes, criminals may use that information to access the account or authorize fraudulent transactions.

Example of a fake bank SMS

“Bank Security” · now

Security alert! Your account will be blocked within 24 hours. Confirm your identity using this link immediately.

{{ICON:flag}} Warning signs

  • Threats that your account will be blocked.
  • Unexpected links asking you to sign in.
  • Requests for your PIN, password, or one-time verification code.
  • Unfamiliar website addresses.
  • Messages that create panic or demand immediate action.

{{ICON:shield}} How to protect yourself

  1. Open your bank’s official app directly instead of using the message link.
  2. Contact your bank using the number on its official website or bank card.
  3. Never share your PIN, password, or one-time verification codes.
  4. Check transactions regularly and report anything suspicious.
  5. If you have disclosed banking information, contact your bank immediately.

05 · Warning signs

Five warning signsof scam messages.

  1. 1Urgency

    The message tells you to act immediately or threatens negative consequences.

  2. 2Fear

    It claims that your parcel will be returned or your bank account will be blocked.

  3. 3Suspicious links

    The website address is unfamiliar, misspelled, or different from the company’s official domain.

  4. 4Requests for sensitive information

    The sender asks for passwords, PINs, card details, or verification codes.

  5. 5Unexpected payments

    The message asks you to pay a fee you were not expecting.

Remember: A familiar company name, logo, or professional-looking message does not prove that the sender is genuine.

{{ICON:buoy}} What if you already clicked the link?

Do not panic. Take action quickly.

  • If you entered banking details or payment information, contact your bank immediately.
  • If you entered a password, change it through the official website or app.
  • If you installed an unknown application, seek trusted technical help to remove it safely.
  • Check your bank account for unauthorized transactions and report them immediately.
  • Save the message and relevant evidence if you need to report the incident.

07 · Why it matters

Scams can affectanyone.

Scam messages can affect anyone, regardless of age, education, or experience. Students, workers, older adults, and online shoppers can all be targeted.

Learning to recognize scams helps us protect our money, identity, and private information. It also allows us to warn friends and family about suspicious messages.

Being careful online does not mean being afraid of every message. It means knowing how to check information before trusting it.

08 · Conclusion

Fake courier and bank SMS messages are common examples of how criminals use technology and social engineering to deceive people. The most important protection is to stay calm, recognize warning signs, and verify messages through trusted channels.

Before clicking any suspicious link, ask yourself:

  • Was I expecting this message?
  • Is the sender genuine?
  • Does the link belong to the official company?
  • Can I verify the information independently?

Stop. Pause. Verify.

Protect your money. Protect your identity. Protect yourself online.
