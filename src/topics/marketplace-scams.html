01 · The key point

They don’t wantyour item.

In most of these scams the buyer isn’t local and doesn’t want your item at all. They want your money.

Below are two real stories, the most common tricks, a checklist to protect yourself, the red flags to watch for, and a game to test what you’ve learned.

02 · Real story

The buyer whopaid too easily.

A fake buyer paid through UPI, then had their bank reverse the payment, leaving the seller with nothing.

I once listed a piece of furniture on OLX, an online marketplace in India. Almost immediately, someone messaged me saying they wanted to buy it. They said they were in Pune and would send someone that evening to pick it up. They offered to pay the full amount upfront and asked for my UPI ID (India’s instant mobile payment system).

I offered to send more photos and asked if they had any questions, but they said no, they trusted me. It all felt too easy, so I ignored them.

A few months later, the exact same thing happened to a friend, and she went ahead with it. Here’s how the scam worked:

  1. The “buyer” sent the payment through UPI.
  2. Then they filed a reversal claim with their bank, saying the payment was a mistake or unauthorized.
  3. The bank refunded the money to the scammer.
  4. My friend was left without the money, and the scammer had already collected the item or the money she’d sent on.
  5. She lost over 1 lakh rupees (₹100,000).

{{ICON:flag}} Red flags in this story

  • Replied right after the listing went up
  • Buyer was in another city and sending “someone” to collect
  • Offered full payment upfront without seeing the item
  • No questions about the item at all
  • Asked for your payment details right away
LessonA payment showing up in your account doesn’t mean it’s final. Wait until the buyer is in front of you, and be suspicious of anyone who wants to pay before even looking.Also: you never need to enter your UPI PIN to receive money. If you’re asked to, it’s a scam.

03 · Real story

The bicycledeposit.

A seller asked for half the price upfront through MB Way, then blocked the buyer and kept the money.

I wanted to buy a bicycle on Facebook Marketplace. The seller said I had to pay half the price upfront through MB Way so he could deliver it to me. As soon as I sent the money, I realized it was a scam, but it was too late. He had already blocked me. Even worse, he was a teenager using his real Facebook account, with all his personal information visible.

I called my bank, but they said they couldn’t help. They did give me the scammer’s name and his account number at Crédito Agrícola. His bank also refused to help and told me to go to the police.

I filed a fraud complaint on the PSP website, which said I had to go to a police station within 48 hours to verify my identity. When I went, the officer refused to verify me and said PSP headquarters in Lisbon would contact me. I went back the next day and showed him the website’s instructions, but he still told me to wait. The 48 hours passed, and my complaint expired.

{{ICON:flag}} Red flags in this story

  • Seller asked for a deposit before delivery
  • Payment by instant transfer (MB Way), which can’t be reversed
  • Delivery instead of meeting in person
LessonNever pay upfront for something you haven’t seen. Once an instant transfer is sent, banks and police often can’t recover it.

04 · Know the tricks

Commonmarketplace scams.

  • 01Overpayment / fake check

    Buyer sends a check for more than the price and asks you to send the extra to their “courier” or “movers.” The check bounces, and the money you sent is gone.

  • 02Fake payment email

    Buyer insists on a specific payment method, then sends a fake PayPal (or similar) email saying you must “upgrade to a business account” or pay a fee to release funds.

  • 03Stolen-funds deposit

    Buyer sends a “deposit” by Venmo/Zelle to hold the item, never shows up, and asks for a refund. The original money was stolen, so the bank holds you responsible. A common line: “Kindly mark your post as SOLD.”

  • 04Counterfeit cash

    Buyer shows up in person, but the bills are fake.

  • 05Shipping / out-of-state scam

    Buyer “lives out of state” and needs you to pay movers or shipping, usually tied to a fake check.

  • 06Verification code scam★ Top tip

    Buyer asks you to read back a code sent to your phone “to prove you’re real.” It’s actually used to hijack your accounts or set up a Google Voice number in your name.

  • 07Phishing link★ Top tip

    Buyer sends a link to “confirm payment” or “arrange delivery” that steals your login or card details.

  • 08Buyer-side scams★ Top tip

    Fake listings (cars, rentals, pets, concert tickets) where the “seller” asks for a deposit and disappears.

06 · Red flags

Stop and thinkwhen a buyer…

  • !Replies within minutes of posting
  • !Asks about “the item” without naming it
  • !Buying it “for a cousin/brother”
  • !A courier or relative will pick it up
  • !Insists on shipping instead of local pickup
  • !Wants to pay right away so you won’t sell to anyone else
  • !Pushes for an urgent response
  • !Asks you to move the chat to text, WhatsApp or email ★ Top tip
  • !Brand-new or empty profile ★ Top tip
