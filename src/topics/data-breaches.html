01 · Story

It can happento anyone.

Imagine you receive an email from a service you use regularly. A few days later, you notice unusual messages and login alerts.

The company announces that it has suffered a data breach. Some customer information may have been exposed, and criminals might try to use it in phishing messages or account takeovers.

You did not cause the breach, but you can still take steps to protect yourself.

02 · Explanation

What is adata breach?

A data breach happens when information is accessed, disclosed, changed, lost, or stolen without authorization. It can affect companies, schools, public institutions, and individuals.

Exposed data may include names, email addresses, phone numbers, passwords, financial details, or private documents. Breaches can result from cyberattacks, weak security, mistakes, or lost devices.

{{ICON:flag}} Why is a data breach dangerous?

  • Criminals may use exposed details to impersonate you.
  • Stolen passwords can put other accounts at risk, especially if passwords are reused.
  • Personal information can be used in targeted phishing or identity fraud.
  • A breach can cause financial loss, stress, and loss of privacy.

{{ICON:shield}} How to stay safe

  1. Use strong, unique passwords for every important account.
  2. Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA/2FA), especially for email and banking.
  3. Keep your operating system, apps, and browser updated.
  4. Be careful with unexpected messages, links, and attachments.
  5. If a service reports a breach, follow its official guidance and change affected or reused passwords.
  6. Check account activity and enable login alerts where available.
  7. If financial details may have been exposed, contact your bank through an official channel.

03 · What to do

If your datamay have leaked.

{{ICON:buoy}} Act in this order

  1. Start with the account affected by the breach. Change its password through the genuine website or app, and change the same password anywhere else you reused it.
  2. Sign out of unfamiliar sessions, enable multi-factor authentication, and watch for unexpected login alerts.
  3. Do not trust follow-up messages just because they mention the breach; criminals may use the news to send fake support links.
  4. If money or payment details are involved, contact your bank immediately.

04 · Practical guide

Have IBeen Pwned?

Have I Been Pwned (HIBP) is a website that lets you check whether an email address appears in data breaches that have been added to its database.

It can help you understand whether an account may be at risk and what steps to take next. It does not prove that an account is currently hacked, and it cannot guarantee that an address has never been exposed.

How to check an email address

  1. Open the official website

    Type haveibeenpwned.com into your browser yourself. Check the spelling before continuing.

  2. Find the email search box

    On the homepage, look for the field asking you to check whether an email address has been involved in a breach.

  3. Enter an email you are allowed to check

    Use your own address, or get the owner’s permission. Do not enter someone else’s email without permission.

  4. Select “Check”

    The website will search its known breach records and display a result.

  5. Read the result carefully

    If a breach is found, review the breach name, date, and types of data reported as exposed.

  6. Take protective action

    Follow the checklist below, especially if passwords or other sensitive information may have been exposed.
